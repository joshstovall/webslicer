# Support-contact volumes

Select an object → **Object modifiers**. Add a cube, cylinder or sphere, or import a closed STL/OBJ/3MF mesh. Choose **Enforce support contacts** (green) or **Block support contacts** (red). Position the volume across the underside to control. Exact local position, rotation and scale, enabled state, overlap priority and apply/cancel use the same editor as infill modifiers. Later support volumes win overlapping contacts. Infill settings have independent priority.

In **Print setup → Support & adhesion**, choose **Automatic · normal** to edit automatically detected contacts or **Enforced only** to generate contacts solely from enforcers. Enforcers ignore the automatic overhang-angle threshold, but only apply to newly exposed undersides. They do not create supports inside solid geometry. An enabled enforcer with supports disabled fails preflight with an actionable message. Blockers alone are harmless when supports are disabled.

These are contact masks: blockers remove initiation areas before support columns are projected downward. A blocker below an underside does not cut an existing column into floating pieces. Supports from an unblocked higher underside can pass through the same volume. Cover the contact area to prevent that support. Native support spacing, XY clearance, density, dedicated filament and adhesion still apply. Surface brush painting and branching tree supports remain unfinished.

## Geometry and persistence

Support volumes follow their owner and linked material parts. Hidden owners and disabled masks are excluded. Unrelated objects remain unaffected. The shared modifier data retains transforms/effect through duplication, assembly changes, material-preserving cuts/splits, undo/redo, IndexedDB recovery and portable projects. Existing projects without a modifier effect retain infill behavior. Closed-volume validation and the combined 32-volume/500,000-triangle limit apply to all effects.

The geometry worker validates enabled volumes before slicing. The bridge routes infill and support modifiers to separate annotations and converts vertices to numeric arrays for the native annotation copier. `support-modifiers.js` intersects volumes with the upper slice's new underside at its sampling plane, applies ordered enforcement/subtraction to the lower slice's contact seeds, then lets the native support projection/fill pipeline run. Fixed, adaptive and manual schedules use their actual sampling planes. Thin features may miss a sampling plane; enlarge the mask across the underside when necessary. Supports are generated on the native non-belt pipeline used by the application's printer profiles.

The incremental patch `scripts/patch-support-modifiers.py` reproduces from `56f0778`. The same patch fixes fixed-layer export rounding: aligned model/support physical tops use the first-layer height plus layer index times nominal height, retaining raft offsets. Previously rounding the sampling midpoint to hundredths could export a 0.25 mm first layer at 0.255 mm. Adaptive/manual schedules retain their existing exact physical tops. Worker/helper checksums are in `vendor-manifest.json`.

Support generation also accounts for other printable parts. The bridge selects neighboring solids whose XY bounds can affect the current object's supports. The worker slices their physical footprints on the shared grid, removes covered contacts, stops downward projections at those solids, and includes their expanded footprints in support clearance and adjacent-layer gaps. This prevents automatic and enforced supports from extruding through lower material regions or neighboring objects. A stacked two-color regression reproduced 168 spurious support segments before the fix; it now produces none. A separate elevated roof/post fixture checks that columns land above the post, retain XY clearance below its top, and still reach the bed elsewhere.

## Tree-support audit correction

The pinned engine's `processSupports` reads `sliceSupportTree` but contains no branching implementation. Earlier tests asserted support existence and tool selection for that flag; they did not prove tree geometry. New selections now offer normal automatic or enforced-only support. Existing saved `tree` settings remain readable and visibly identify their normal output. True branching supports remain part of the broader goal. This corrects earlier normal/tree capability claims without treating compatibility as completion.

Primary implementation evidence: pinned [FDM support generation](https://github.com/GridSpace/grid-apps/blob/d138275bbe9d4e4030d8cb8b2dcf66607da9295a/src/kiri/mode/fdm/work/slice.js) and [support preparation](https://github.com/GridSpace/grid-apps/blob/d138275bbe9d4e4030d8cb8b2dcf66607da9295a/src/kiri/mode/fdm/work/prepare.js).
